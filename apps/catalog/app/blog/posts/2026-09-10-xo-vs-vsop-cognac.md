An XO Cognac typically costs two to three times what a VSOP from the same house does. Our [Cognac buying guide](/blog/best-cognac-bangkok) covers what the age grades legally mean — this post answers the harder question: is the XO actually worth the difference, or is the VSOP the smarter buy? Like most of our [spirits guides](/blog/category/guides), the answer depends less on the bottles than on what you plan to do with them.

## The Label Is a Minimum, Not a Description

VSOP means the youngest spirit in the blend spent at least 4 years in French oak. XO means at least 10 — a bar that was raised from 6 years in 2018, precisely because the old minimum had drifted too far below what serious houses were actually bottling. That gap matters: the grades describe the floor, not the blend.

In practice, a good house's VSOP contains spirit well older than 4 years, and its XO reaches well past 10. So when you pay the XO premium, you're not buying a number on a label — you're buying a fundamentally different stage of the spirit's life.

![Oak barrels resting in a dark cellar](https://images.pexels.com/photos/2946621/pexels-photo-2946621.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Those extra years change the liquid in ways you can taste blind. Young Cognac is about fruit — grape, apricot, fresh oak spice. Somewhere past the decade mark, oxidation and slow extraction from the barrel push it toward what the Cognacais call *rancio*: dried fig, leather, walnut, dark toffee, a savory depth that no young spirit can fake. VSOP is fruit with structure. XO is time made drinkable.

VSOP is the grade you can use without ceremony. It's complex enough to sip neat, affordable enough to mix, and forgiving enough to pour for guests who won't give it fifteen minutes in a tulip glass.

## The Case for XO: When Only Depth Will Do

XO earns its price in exactly one scenario: slow, attentive drinking. Neat pours, after dinner, real glassware, no mixers. If that's how the bottle will actually be used — or if it's a serious gift — the premium is money well spent.

<!-- product: LBD0251HK -->
**Michel Forgeron Cognac XO Carafe** — ฿8,579

A small family house rather than a global brand — the XO arrives in a hand-finished carafe and tastes like an individual's decisions rather than a committee's: dried fig, cocoa, a long, dry finish. The connoisseur's pick, and the gift for someone who already owns the famous names.

**Martell Chanteloup XXO** — ฿27,999

Beyond XO sits XXO — a grade formally recognized in 2018 requiring a minimum of 14 years — and Martell's Chanteloup is one of the few examples in Bangkok at all. Deep rancio character, immense length, from the oldest of the big four houses. This is trophy territory: a milestone-occasion bottle in the same spirit as a prestige vintage Champagne.

## So Which Should You Buy?

Buy VSOP if the bottle will live an active life — cocktails, casual pours, weeknight drinking, or your first exploration of the category. At ฿2,400–3,600 in Bangkok, it delivers most of Cognac's character at a price where you'll actually open it. Buy XO when the bottle has one clear job: contemplative neat drinking or a gift that needs to land with weight. And if you're choosing between one XO or two great VSOPs plus change, remember the Camus Borderies pair above — tasting the two grades side by side will teach you more than any label ever will. The same logic drives every good spirits comparison, whether it's [Speyside against Islay](/blog/speyside-vs-islay-scotch-whisky-debate) or aged expressions in our [rum guide](/blog/rum-guide-bangkok): match the bottle to how it will really be drunk, not to the biggest number you can afford.

## FAQ

### Is XO Cognac better than VSOP?
XO is older, deeper, and more complex, but not automatically "better." VSOP (minimum 4 years in oak) is brighter and more versatile — it works neat and in cocktails. XO (minimum 10 years) trades that freshness for dried fruit, leather, and rancio depth best appreciated neat. The better bottle is the one that matches how you'll drink it.

### Is XO Cognac worth the extra money?
If you drink Cognac neat and slowly, yes — the extra decade in oak produces flavors a VSOP cannot reach at any price. If the bottle will mostly go into cocktails or casual pours, a quality VSOP delivers more enjoyment per baht, and the XO premium is largely wasted.

### What does XO mean on a Cognac label?
XO stands for "Extra Old." Since 2018, it legally requires the youngest spirit in the blend to have aged at least 10 years in French oak — raised from the previous 6-year minimum. Most quality houses blend well beyond the legal floor.

### Can you use XO Cognac in cocktails?
You can, but it's rarely worth it. Mixers and citrus flatten the aged complexity that the XO premium pays for. Classic Cognac cocktails like the Sidecar were built around younger spirit — a good VSOP such as H by Hine gives a better result in the glass at a third of the price.

### What is XXO Cognac?
XXO ("Extra Extra Old") is a grade formally recognized in 2018 for blends whose youngest component has aged at least 14 years. It sits above XO as the oldest official age designation in Cognac — Martell's Chanteloup XXO is one of the few examples available in Bangkok.
